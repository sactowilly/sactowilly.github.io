How I work

Build the right thing before building more things.

AI can generate output quickly. The difficult part is deciding what should be built, what information it may use, how success is measured, and where a person must remain accountable.

“The objective is not more AI. It is better work.”
  1. 01

    Discover

    Identify the user, the decision, the current friction, the constraints, and the actual outcome that matters.

  2. 02

    Structure

    Turn messy requirements into a clear workflow, information model, guardrails, ownership map, and acceptance criteria.

  3. 03

    Build

    Use AI-assisted development to produce the smallest complete version that can be tested with real use cases.

  4. 04

    Test and refine

    Check calculations, privacy, usability, edge cases, failure modes, and whether the result actually improves the work.

  5. 05

    Document and deploy

    Make the system understandable, maintainable, and transferable instead of leaving behind a fragile demonstration.

About Will

An experienced operator who learned to build.

I came to AI through business, not through a traditional software-engineering career. I have spent decades working in leadership, sales, operations, organizational development, and nonprofit management—where the hard problems are usually part process, part people, part information, and part technology.

AI-assisted development gave me a way to move beyond identifying problems and writing recommendations. I can now translate requirements into prototypes, test them, revise them, document them, and put working tools in front of the people who need them.

I hold a bachelor’s degree in business management, an MBA, a Master of Nonprofit Management, and completed doctoral coursework in organizational development. I am also a U.S. Air Force veteran based in Sacramento.

Working principles

  • Outcome before output.Start with what needs to improve.
  • People stay accountable.Important decisions retain human approval.
  • Privacy is architectural.Data boundaries are defined before deployment.
  • Test what can fail.Edge cases matter more than perfect demos.
  • Document the system.A usable handoff is part of the product.
